\section{Experimental Protocol, Revised}
\label{sec:protocol}

No language model has been trained. This section specifies the experiments that would settle what Section~\ref{sec:studies} could not, revised in four places where the studies showed the original design was wrong or uninformative. Revisions are marked. All result tables in Section~\ref{sec:placeholders} are placeholders and contain no data.

\subsection{E0: the diagnostic that decides the RoPE contribution}
\label{sec:e0}

Take a pretrained RoPE model. For each layer, extract key vectors both before and after the rotary embedding is applied, cluster each set on the sphere, and measure what the clusters encode.

\begin{revision}{Revised by S1, S2 and the synthetic run}
The original specification led with $I(\text{cluster};\text{position})$. Two corrections. First, that quantity is \emph{non-monotone} in context length --- in a synthetic run with all confounds removed it rose to $0.38$ at $n = 256$, fell to $0.13$ at $n = 4096$, and was $0.16$ at $n = 16{,}384$ --- because at short context the phase correlates with position and at long context it scrambles, while the damage grows throughout. The quantity that falls monotonically is $I(\text{cluster};\text{token identity})$, from $1.00$ without RoPE to $0.50$ at $n = 16{,}384$, and it should be the headline. Second, E0 must report \textbf{effective bucket count}, because the failure S2 identifies is a capacity ceiling rather than uniform hashing, and mutual information does not see it.

A third point follows from S2 and is easy to get wrong: the ceiling is a property of \emph{fixed-codebook argmax} routing. Spherical $k$-means fits its centroids to wherever the data went and does not exhibit it --- in the synthetic run the effective cluster count stayed near $29$ of $32$ under RoPE while the argmax router collapsed to five. \textbf{E0 must therefore be run with both clustering rules}, or it will measure the wrong failure.
\end{revision}

\paragraph{Specification.} Models: Pythia-160m/410m or a comparable open RoPE model; the hook must capture keys on both sides of \texttt{apply\_rotary\_pos\_emb}, and the two tensors must be asserted to differ before any number is trusted. Data: WikiText-103 or FineWeb-Edu. Clustering: spherical $k$-means with $k$-means++ seeding and three restarts, \emph{and} fixed-codebook argmax, at $B \in \{16, 64, 256\}$. Contexts: $512$ to the model's maximum. Report $I(\text{cluster};\text{token})$, $I(\text{cluster};\text{position})$ and effective bucket count, split by RoPE frequency tercile.

\paragraph{Falsification.} If pre- and post-RoPE keys cluster comparably on real data, Proposition~\ref{prop:rope} has no practical force at the scales that matter and the RoPE-decoupling contribution should be withdrawn, not reformulated.

\subsection{Models, data and matching}

Two scales, trained from scratch: \textbf{S} ($\sim$125M non-embedding parameters, 12 layers, $d=768$, 12 heads) and \textbf{M} ($\sim$350M, 24 layers, $d=1024$, 16 heads). Data: FineWeb-Edu \citep{penedo2024fineweb}, with WikiText-103 \citep{merity2017pointer} and PG-19 \citep{rae2020compressive} for long-context evaluation. Token budgets follow compute-optimal ratios \citep{hoffmann2022chinchilla}. Contexts $\{1\text{k}, 4\text{k}, 16\text{k}, 32\text{k}\}$.

\begin{revision}{Added by S7}
Remark~\ref{rem:coupling} means the routing width cannot be held fixed across the context sweep without changing what is being measured: at $\dc = 32$ the method is at its claimed operating point at $n = 4096$ and is quadratic-with-small-constant at $n = 32{,}768$. Runs must therefore either scale $\dc$ as $\sqrt{n}$, or hold $\dc$ fixed and report that the complexity regime changes across the sweep. Reporting a single speed curve across contexts at fixed $\dc$ would be misleading.
\end{revision}

Every baseline is matched on total parameters, parameters active per token, training FLOPs and training tokens. Runs that cannot be matched on all four are reported separately and labelled. \textbf{Every table states what it is matched on}; the released harness records this in the run metadata so it cannot be left implicit.

\subsection{Baselines}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Dense transformer with FlashAttention \citep{dao2022flashattention} and full RoPE.
\item Dense transformer with partial RoPE at the same $\rho = \dpos/d_h$, which isolates the effect of the split from the effect of routing.
\item Reformer LSH attention \citep{kitaev2020reformer} at matched bucket count and rounds.
\item Routing Transformer \citep{roy2021routing} at matched cluster count.
\item Sliding window with global tokens \citep{beltagy2020longformer} at matched density.
\item Mixture-of-Depths \citep{raposo2024mixture} at matched capacity --- the baseline for the fallback claim.
\item NSA \citep{yuan2025nsa} and/or MoBA \citep{lu2025moba} where a faithful reimplementation is feasible; if not, state so rather than omitting silently.
\end{enumerate}

Baselines 1--6 are reachable in the released implementation by changing one flag, so A1, A3 and A4 are single-variable interventions rather than comparisons between separately written systems that differ in a dozen incidental ways.

\subsection{Metrics}

Validation perplexity; downstream zero-shot accuracy; wall-clock training step time and decode latency on a stated GPU; peak memory; KV read bandwidth per decode step; bucket-load entropy $H(\pi)/\log B$ over training; \textbf{effective bucket count} (added by S2); fraction of attention mass on residue summaries; recall of the sparse pattern against dense attention; and \textbf{in-bucket recall of the chunked kernel} (added by S7), without which a wall-clock number describes a different function from the one the method defines.

\begin{revision}{Added by the toy sweep}
Report \textbf{loss trajectories, not only final numbers}, and run every arm until the dense baseline has \emph{plateaued} rather than merely started to learn. On the toy task the onset of the learning transition varied by more than a factor of two across seeds of an identical configuration, and a budget truncated near that onset makes every slower arm report at the floor --- which looks like a decisive negative result and is not one. Seeds must be sufficient to resolve onset-time variance, which is a different and larger requirement than resolving steady-state variance.
\end{revision}

\subsection{Predictions}

We commit in advance, so that what remains is falsifiable. P1 and P3 are restated from their original form in light of the studies.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[P1$'$.] A3 shows a perplexity gap favouring content-dimension routing that \emph{widens} with context length, and the gap correlates with the effective-bucket-count gap rather than with mutual information against position. (Original P1 predicted a widening gap tracked by $I(\text{cluster};\text{position})$; S1 showed that metric is non-monotone.)
\item[P2.] A1 shows learned $\bR$ beating random fixed $\bR$ at equal $B$ and $n_r$, with the gap largest at small $B$.
\item[P3$'$.] A4 shows residue summaries beating a pure skip at equal FLOPs, and in the \emph{converged} model at residue bias $-4$ the attention mass on summaries lies between $2\%$ and $25\%$. (Original P3 omitted both the convergence qualifier and the bias, which S6 showed makes the band settable and the prediction unfalsifiable.)
\item[P4.] A6$'$ shows whitening the routing input restoring load entropy under anisotropic encoder output, where router-side $\lambda$ does not.
\item[P5.] Wall-clock gains appear only above a crossover length --- we guess 8k--16k at scale S on current hardware --- and PHASOR is \emph{slower} than dense FlashAttention below it. Any such measurement is reported with in-bucket recall alongside.
\item[P6.] \emph{New, from S7.} In-bucket recall above $90\%$ requires $c \gtrsim n/B$, and holding $\dc$ fixed while growing $n$ produces a measurable perplexity degradation relative to scaling $\dc \propto \sqrt{n}$ at matched FLOPs.
\end{enumerate}
