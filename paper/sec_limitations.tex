\section{Limitations and Open Problems}
\label{sec:open}

The failure modes in Section~\ref{sec:limits} were written before the studies. This section states where the design stands now, in rough order of how likely each is to sink it.

\paragraph{1. The width--context coupling (Remark~\ref{rem:coupling}).} The $O(n^{1.5})$ operating point requires $\dc \approx \sqrt{n}/2$, because $B = 2\dc$ and chunk fidelity requires $c \gtrsim n/B$. At fixed routing width the method is quadratic with a constant factor. This is the most consequential limitation and it is structural: it follows from the router's definition, not from an implementation choice. Multi-round routing is the obvious relaxation and is unmeasured. Decoupling $B$ from $\dc$ --- for instance by composing several independent cross-polytope partitions of disjoint subspaces, which would multiply the effective codebook without widening any single rotation --- is the design change we would try first, and it is not in this paper.

\paragraph{2. Load collapse, which is the encoder's to cause and to fix.} Proposition~\ref{prop:load} guarantees balance for isotropic routing inputs and guarantees nothing otherwise, and S3 shows the transition is sharp: by $\kappa = 4$ the load entropy is $0.10$. Whether a trained encoder's key representations are close enough to isotropic in the routing subspace is an empirical question nobody has answered, and the answer decides whether the $n^2/B$ accounting holds at all. If they are strongly anisotropic, whitening is the remedy and it has its own costs, which A6$'$ would measure.

\paragraph{3. Rigidity may cost accuracy.} A fixed equiangular partition is less expressive than data-fit centroids. Routing Transformer's $k$-means allocates resolution where the data is dense; PHASOR cannot, except by moving the whole frame rigidly. If the data manifold is anisotropic this is a real disadvantage --- and note it is the same condition that causes problem 2, so the two failure modes are correlated rather than independent. A2 measures the cost.

\paragraph{4. The fixed-hash null.} \citet{roller2021hash} show random fixed hashing is a strong router, and S5 now shows that before training our learned router and a frozen random one produce patterns of identical quality. The entire value of the learned frame therefore rests on what training does to it, with no cheaper evidence available. If A1 shows no gap, the central claim of this paper is false and should be reported as such.

\paragraph{5. Semantic specialisation probably will not appear.} The intuition motivating this line of work is that buckets will correspond to linguistically meaningful categories. Evidence from MoE models suggests expert specialisation tends to be syntactic and positional rather than semantic \citep{jiang2024mixtral}, and probing work shows linguistic structure is distributed across layers rather than concentrated \citep{tenney2019bert,clark2019bertlook}. We rest no efficiency claim on interpretability of buckets; A7 measures it as a descriptive question and a negative result there does not falsify the method.

\paragraph{6. Hardware.} Gather and scatter over sorted buckets have poor arithmetic intensity relative to dense FlashAttention \citep{dao2022flashattention}. Reformer and Routing Transformer both report that theoretical FLOP savings translate imperfectly into wall-clock, and NSA \citep{yuan2025nsa} exists largely because of this. Every table must report wall-clock, not only FLOPs --- and now also in-bucket recall, since a fast kernel that computes a different function is not a speedup.

\paragraph{7. The residue branch is under-specified and over-free.} D1 and D2 in Section~\ref{sec:design-decisions} are choices we made without evidence, and S6 shows one of them spans two orders of magnitude of behaviour. The branch's contribution to the method is currently the least constrained part of the design.

\paragraph{8. What we have not measured at all.} Decoding behaviour, including whether the running summaries drift relative to a full recomputation over long generations; interaction with grouped-query attention, where the routing decision would have to be shared across a key--value group; behaviour under a trained rather than randomly initialised encoder, which is the regime every claim in this paper actually concerns; and anything at all above toy scale.
